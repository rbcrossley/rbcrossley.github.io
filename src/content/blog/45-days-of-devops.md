Learning DevOps properly can take years. I am not claiming to learn DevOps in 45 days. 

And what is DevOps? I listened to a wide range of folks in the industry. It differs from place to place.

What most people think DevOps is that it means working with servers. Basically we think they are sysadmin+CI/CD.

But that is not the end of DevOps.

If you are working in a small shop, then DevOps could include network engineering, network administration, database administration, and support engineering (alongside sysadmin+CI/CD work).



# Success
The success of the series for me will be measured as
- some projects 
- a sense of confidence (very subjective thing) and fluency in DevOps

# How I am learning?
I will be using resources on demand. Udemy courses will be my goto source. Along with youtube and articles available online. I will use almost everything available for free. 

University slides are also helpful if they exist for the topic I am looking to study.


Chip Huyen's "AI Engineering" book is something that I will be buying.


I have picked two udemy courses. One is on LEMP on Rocky Linux 10 and another is about Jenkins CI/CD.


I have started the LEMP udemy course and at this point, I find it too command copy-paste type. No knowledge about how stuffs work behind the scene or some architectural viewpoint is being provided. Simply commands are listed and we are asked to enter them on our server.

The Jenkins CI/CD course is going well. 


The roadmap that I will be following will be:

1) network administration

I will learn to use some open source firewalls like pfSense or OPNsense. Since nepali ISPs do not allow port forwarding on residential IPs, I think learning firewall is going to come hard by. The udemy courses that exist in this field are pretty bland. 

2) database administration

I have Ahmed Baraka's Oracle SQL course on Udemy, I will get through it. If I ever finish it(more than 60% completion), I will make a review of that course. It is very technically demanding course from what I have seen so far.

3) CI CD

I will continue the Jenkins CI/CD course. My goal is to pick the fundamentals. Not the tooling. Jenkins is probably being phasesd out by gitlab CI/CD. Applications keep changing but the core mechanisms behind the technology never changes. My goal is to learn the core technology.


4) AI

Read the Chip Huyen's book on AI. Once I get confused on it and feel like I need more resources, buy/seek those resources and start learning from them.

5) Containerization

I currently do not know how I will learn it, but I guess I will do it while learning LLMOps or stuffs like that. Patience is a virtue because in the future some good courses might arrive in this field or my brain might be technologically matured enough to grasp these concepts.
