# Exam Pattern

Based on the official syllabus, the written examination consists of 200 marks followed by an interview.
The first paper is of 100 marks. It is divided into first 45 minutes and next 1 hour 30 minutes exams.
The second paper is of 100 marks. It is of 3 hours.

If you clear both first and second paper, you will enter interview stage. Interview is of 30 marks.

> Note that there is negative marking in MCQs. 20% marks are deducted for every 1 wrong answer.

> Note that the age limit to apply is 35 years for male, and 40 years for female.

To get qualified you will need 40 marks each in first and second paper. But your name will be published by PSC only if you are in the topper list. 

The topper list means that if three seats are open, then there will be around 9 toppers who will be called for interview. 

**Paper I (100 Marks)**
- General Awareness and Contemporary Issues (MCQs):30 marks
- Mathematics and Statistics(MCQs):20 marks
- Management & Institutional Awareness (Subjective):50 marks

**Paper II (100 Marks)**
The subjects for Computer Engineering in second paper are as follows:
Section A:
(It is of 50 marks. It consists of 2 short questions worth 5 marks each, and 4 long questions worth 10 marks each.)
- digital signal processing
- digital systems design
- computer architectures
- principles of VLSI
- computer communications
- artificial intelligence and expert systems
- distributed systems and cloud computing

Section B:
(It is of 50 marks. It consists of 2 short questions worth 5 marks each, and 4 long questions worth 10 marks each.)
- program design and data structures
- operating systems
- software engineering
- databases and file systems
- internet programming
- client server computing
- cryptography and network security
- basic electricity and electronics

This is entirely technical and subjective.

There is no written priority or weightage of subjects given officially by NTC. So, each subjects hold equal priority.

# Government bank vs NTC job in Nepal

You might be wondering, government bank or NTC which government job is better in Nepal.

First if I had to joke a bit, crack one and see yourself. I say this because cracking a government job is not an easy feat. Specially if you were rusty in your Bachelors Degree.

First let's be clear. Unless you have your Masters in IT/Engineering degree, you will have to go with only Level 5 posts in government banks, except Nepal Rastra Bank.

Level 5 means upto Sr. Assistant Level, i.e., not officer level. Although government bank pays really good+loans facilities are there as well.


Read my blog on RBB Sr. IT Assistant preparation:

https://berojgarengineer.com/blog/rbb-fifth-level-senior-assistant-it-vacancy-job-roadmap/

However in Nepal Rastra Bank you can work at Level 6, Assistant Director IT.


Read the following blog if you want to learn more about NRB vacancies opening this year, best books, syllabus, and preparation guidance.

https://berojgarengineer.com/blog/nrb-vacancy/

So you are comparing between three companies:

NTC vs Class A government bank(Nepal Bank, RBB, ADBL) vs Regulatory bank (NRB).

Lots of people simply like to think that NRB job is the best. They think so because of what they hear from others online. But they forget the part that NRB's job is regulatory. It is a completely boring work at NRB. At least for me, I cannot imagine myself working in RegTech for the rest of my life. The work at class A banks is more interesting and more fun. 

If you are a core computer engineer, then NTC job might be better. The reason I say so is that although currently NTC uses its computer engineer as IT personnels, in the future this will definitely change. From Artificial Intelligence Engineering, and Machine Learning Engineering to anything that requires engineering degree, NTC will use computer engineers that way for sure. Computer Engineering will be the core aspect of NTC in the future.

For banks, IT is just a support part. It is an operational part that banks will be happy if they do not have to do. They want to reduce their footprint as much as possible. I am not saying bank IT jobs do not pay well though. They pay great, and everything is super good at banks based on what I have researched so far and heard from others. 

Most of the tasks at banks is handled by vendors anyway. Your task is just to co-ordinate with other parties, and maybe handle some Core Banking System reporting via Oracle SQL queries.


For example, the mobile banking app you use is made by F1soft. They write the code, handle the production support and feature release aspect of this app which is like a core customer side function provided by banks these days. 

If you are in bank, it will want to get rid of you one day. It will just plan its operations that way. But if you are in NTC, they will want to extract maximum value from you because you are NTC's growth booster.

Obviously, the work will be challenging at NTC. However in class 'A' banks, you will have to do End Of Day operations as IT personnel.

You will have to wake up till 1 AM. It can be a deal breaker for some. But it is not always. It is on round-robin basis for some days. For me it is not. Because I can sleep during the day as it is a leave for the day.

I am not trying to demotivate you from your NRB aspirations though. It pays the best in Nepal because it is boring job. Neither I am trying to demotivate you from bank's job. I personally prefer bank's job due to loan facilities and other opportunities.

Note that although I said banks will try to get rid of you. But in government banks, they cannot get rid of you that easily without any serious reasons. So no need to worry about that. 

And growth will be there by default via internal competition. But I am telling you about the real prospects to grow as a computer engineer in nepal. 

And not be a job only to earn money but to change the landscape of Communications and Information Technology in Nepal.

But believe it or not. Cracking NTC is not a cakewalk. Bank's 5th level is pretty easy as a Bachelors graduate. But NTC syllabus itself is so tough. Imagine cracking that exam.
